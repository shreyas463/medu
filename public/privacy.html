Medu

Privacy

11 November 2026

Medu is a hieroglyph reader. It has no account, no login, and no database of its own. This page is the full list of where information can sit.

On your device

  • The first-visit guide stores one flag in this browser, medu.tour with the value done. It stays on the device. Clearing site data for this site removes it.
  • The signs on the line are written into the page address, as Gardiner codes such as #F35-I9-D21. Medu does not keep a copy of that address. Anyone you send the link to can see those signs.
  • Copy the rubbing places the link on your clipboard. Save rubbing builds a picture in the browser and downloads it. Both stay on your machine.

Visit counts

The published site can send one page view to GoatCounter when the counter is turned on. The local site does not send it. Placing a sign does not send another view. While the counter is off, that request is not made.

When a view is sent, GoatCounter stores aggregate counts, in separate tables:

  • The path and the hour, and the referrer for that hour.
  • A daily total for the browser name and version.
  • A daily total for the system, such as the operating system.
  • A daily total for the country, derived from the IP address.
  • A daily total for the language and for the screen width.

Those tables are not linked to each other. GoatCounter discards the IP address after it has a country, and it discards the full User-Agent after it has a browser and a system. It does not set a cookie, and it does not write to local storage. To avoid counting one person twice, it holds the site name, the IP address, and the User-Agent in memory for up to eight hours, then drops them.

Medu leaves GoatCounter’s individual pageviews setting off. That setting would store one row per view, including a random session id. It stays off.

The counts are stored on GoatCounter’s servers at Hetzner Online GmbH in Finland and Germany. GoatCounter.com is operated by Martin Tournoij in Ireland. GoatCounter does not share that information with a third party. The dashboard belongs to the person who runs Medu. Visitors do not see it.

Who serves the page

The published site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Opening it sends the request, including the IP address, to GitHub. Those host logs are GitHub’s. Their statement is the GitHub General Privacy Statement.

Removing it

There is no Medu account to delete. Clear this site’s data in the browser to remove the guide flag. The person who runs the counter can delete the visit counts in GoatCounter. Deleting the GoatCounter account removes the counts, and backups there can last up to 30 days.
